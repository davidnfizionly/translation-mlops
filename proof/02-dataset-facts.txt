Origin of the texts (assessment, not proof)
- English side: encyclopedic sentences consistent with Wikipedia
  ("is a species of" 8,705 rows; "(born " 22,230 rows; village/town/hamlet
  definitions 5,212 rows).
- All 499,880 English sentences have both a Nuer and a Dinka version, with
  no empty field and no copy of the English: a three-way human translation
  of this size for two low-resource languages is unlikely.
- Degenerate repetition (a word trigram 3 or more times in one sentence):
  Dinka 36,579 rows (7.3%), Nuer 6,688 (1.3%), English source 623 (0.1%).
  This looping pattern is typical of neural machine translation.
- Loops replace content: in the random sample, the Dinka output for a
  sentence about "Pail Shop Corners ... Otsego County, New York" repeats one
  short phrase many times and drops most of the place names.
- Numbers copied exactly: Nuer 96.0%, Dinka 95.0% of 212,652 rows with numbers.
- The same publisher's health dataset (MIT) states on its card that its Nuer
  and Dinka text was produced with Google Translate.
Conclusion: the Nuer and Dinka sides are very probably machine translated
from English (high confidence, not confirmed by the author). The card does
not say how the translations were produced.
